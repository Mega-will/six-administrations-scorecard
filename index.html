    Average American Ledger

    What happened to the typical household's pay, work, prices, poverty, health coverage, housing, lifespan and safety under each president since 1993, with the party that held Congress drawn under every chart. The ledger records who presided over each change. It cannot say who caused it, and the roll-up below shows how quickly the partisan tally moves when four shock years are set aside.

    How to read it

    • Years are assigned to the president in office for most of the year; the January 1–20 overlap is ignored.
    • "Better" thresholds per term: unemployment, prime-age employment, poverty, uninsured and homeownership ±0.5 pt; median income and disposable income ±3%; weekly earnings ±2%; life expectancy ±0.3 yr; overdoses ±10%; violent crime ±5%; debt ±3 pt of GDP. Inflation is the average rate over the term: 2.5% or less is marked better, 3.5% or more worse.
    • Series breaks: Census income and poverty (2013 CPS redesign, 2017 processing change); NHIS uninsured (2019 redesign); FBI crime (2021 NIBRS transition); homeownership (2020 survey disruption). Hover the marked points.
    • Violent crime uses the FBI's legacy rape definition through 2014 and the revised one from 2015, which adds about 10 per 100,000; Obama's cell crosses that splice and understates the fall. The 2025 crime figure is a first release and overdoses for 2025 are provisional. The uninsured series starts in 1997 and overdoses in 1999, so Clinton's cells there cover part of his term.
    • An independent audit on Nov 11, 2026 re-checked about 230 year-points against the source pages and corrected 14 values, most of them FBI crime revisions. On the corrected series violent crime fell slightly under Trump 45 (the first version showed a rise).
    • Pandemic distortions: the 2020 jump in weekly earnings reflects lower-paid workers losing jobs, 2020–21 disposable income includes relief payments, and Census says 2019–20 income and poverty were flattered by survey nonresponse. These affect the Trump 45 endpoint and the Biden starting point.
    • No October 2025 household survey or CPI was collected, so 2025 averages cover 11 months. Inflation is computed from the seasonally adjusted index and can differ from the official annual figure by 0.1; the 2026 point is September's 12-month change. Life expectancy is the World Bank series, 0.1 below the CDC's figure in 2021, 2022 and 2024. Debt is the calendar fourth-quarter value, not the fiscal-year figure.
    • Axes do not start at zero; every gridline is labeled so the range is explicit.

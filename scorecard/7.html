Six Administrations Scorecard · Bucket 7 of 10

Energy, Infrastructure & Environment

U.S. crude output fell under three administrations of both parties, from 6.85 million b/d in 1993 to 5.00 million b/d in 2008, then more than doubled to a record 13.66 million b/d in 2025 under the next three (EIA), a swing driven mostly by private-land shale technology and world prices that every White House since has claimed or disowned.

7.1

Domestic oil and gas production and energy independence

Measures crude and gas output and the net energy trade position; determines price exposure, sanctions leverage and vulnerability to foreign supply shocks.

AdministrationFactsDefenders sayCritics sayAttribution
DClinton (1993–2001)Crude output fell from 6.85 million b/d (1993) to 5.82 million b/d (2000) (EIA); on Feb 16, 1995 Clinton accepted a Commerce finding that import dependence threatened national security but rejected tariffs or quotas, directing DOE to find ways to revive domestic output (UCSB); Deepwater Royalty Relief Act (1995, R Congress) seeded later Gulf production; petroleum imports kept rising every year toward their 2005 peak (EIA).Cheap oil (WTI averaged $14–22/bbl in each year 1993–99, EIA) was a consumer windfall; the administration kept the SPR, pushed efficiency and gas, and deepwater royalty relief paid off a decade later (administration's own record, UCSB).Industry and Republican critics said the 1995 finding was followed by no production strategy, imports kept climbing, and the country was exposed when WTI doubled from $14.42 (1998) to $30.38 (2000) (EIA).D Congress 1993–95, R Congress 1995–2001. Decline reflected depleted conventional fields and $10–20 oil set by OPEC and the Asian crisis, not federal policy; shale techniques developed privately in the late 1990s paid off a decade later.
RBush 43 (2001–2009)Output kept falling to 5.00 million b/d in 2008, the modern low (EIA); EPAct 2005 (signed Aug 8, 2005) gave $2.8B in fossil incentives and exempted fracking fluids from Safe Drinking Water Act rules (Wikipedia); EISA 2007 set a 35 mpg fleet target by 2020 and a 36B-gallon biofuel mandate by 2022 (CRS); the shift toward net petroleum exports began in 2006 (FactCheck.org).EPAct's fracking exemption and expanded leasing laid the legal groundwork for the shale boom; EISA's efficiency and biofuel mandates diversified supply after the "addicted to oil" diagnosis.The Washington Post called EPAct a collection of subsidies for oil and nuclear (Wikipedia); production bottomed and WTI averaged $99.67/bbl in 2008, up from $25.98 in 2001 (EIA), while the fracking exemption became the "Halliburton loophole."R Congress through 2006; EISA passed a D Congress 86–8 (CRS). The 2007–08 price spike was global demand-driven; shale output arrived after he left.
DObama (2009–2017)Output rose from 5.36 million b/d (2009) to 9.43 million (2015), easing to 8.86 million in 2016 (EIA); rejected Keystone XL on Nov 6, 2015 (Wikipedia); signed the Dec 18, 2015 omnibus repealing the 1975 crude export ban, after which exports rose from under 0.5 million b/d (2015) to almost 3 million b/d (2019) (GAO); methane and federal-land fracking rules issued 2015–16 (approx.)."All of the above" produced 40-year production highs, falling imports and gas displacing coal; export-ban repeal was a bipartisan deal (Roll Call-reported Democratic framing, Roll Call).The boom happened on private and state land despite federal leasing limits; Keystone, offshore and Arctic restrictions and methane rules were reversed as "burdens" by the next administration (Trump White House archive).D Congress 2009–11, R House from 2011, R Congress 2015–17 (export repeal was in an R-drafted omnibus). Shale growth tracked $100 oil in 2011–14; the 2015–16 dip followed the Saudi price war.
RTrump 45 (2017–2021)Output rose from 9.37 million b/d (2017) to a record 12.34 million (2019), then fell to 11.35 million in 2020 (EIA); in 2019 gross energy exports (23.6 quads) exceeded imports for the first time since 1952 (EIA); 2020 net petroleum exports of 635,000 b/d, the first annual surplus since at least 1949 (FactCheck.org); Keystone XL permit signed Mar 24, 2017 (Wikipedia); ANWR opened to leasing (Trump White House archive)."Energy dominance": net energy exporter for the first time in nearly 70 years, top oil and gas producer, Clean Power Plan replaced, coal moratorium lifted (Trump White House archive).The net-export milestone continued a trend begun in 2006 and crossed the line only because COVID crushed demand; the U.S. stayed a net crude importer (FactCheck.org); 2020 output fell 1 million b/d.R Congress 2017–19 (ANWR leasing was opened by the Dec 22, 2017 tax law, P.L. 115-97, CRS), D House 2019–21. Permian private capital drove output; the 2020 collapse was COVID; shale momentum was inherited.
DBiden (2021–2025)Output rose from 11.32 million b/d (2021) to 12.97 million (2023) and 13.27 million (2024), successive world records (EIA); revoked the Keystone XL permit Jan 20, 2021 (Wikipedia); BLM approved roughly 6,500 drilling permits in his first two years vs about 6,300 under Trump; the IRA tied renewable leasing to oil and gas lease sales (Roll Call); paused new LNG export approvals in Jan 2024 (the "Biden-era freeze" lifted Feb 14, 2025, AEA); 180-million-barrel emergency SPR release in 2022, with final sales awarded Nov 3, 2022 (DOE).Record output, record permits and record exports refute the "war on energy" charge; the IRA guaranteed lease sales; the LNG pause was a study, not a ban (Roll Call).The leasing pause, delayed offshore plan, LNG pause and pipeline denials constrain output with multi-year lags, and Democrats cannot claim credit for production their coalition opposes (CEI).D Congress 2021–23 (IRA), R House 2023–25. The 2022 price shock was Russia's invasion; output growth came from Permian productivity; the 2021 leasing pause was enjoined by a federal judge in Louisiana on June 15, 2021 in a 13-state suit (KUTV).
RTrump 47 (2025– ) IN PROGRESSJan 20, 2025 declared a "national energy emergency," the first use of that basis for executive powers (E&E News); LNG approvals resumed (Commonwealth Feb 14, 2025; CP2 final Oct 21, 2025) and Congress repealed the methane fee Feb 26, 2025 (AEA); OBBBA (Jul 4, 2025) mandates quarterly onshore lease sales, four ANWR and six NPR-A sales, cuts royalties about $6B over a decade and delays the methane fee 10 years (Wikipedia); 2025 output a record 13.66 million b/d, with EIA projecting about 13.7 million in 2026 (EIA).425 actions have "unleashed" energy: LNG permits, Alaska reopened, quarterly lease sales, a $300M-bid Gulf sale, lower royalties (AEA)."There is no energy emergency": output was already at records when he took office (E&E News); Dallas Fed respondents say tariff and price swings make it "hard to drill, baby, drill" (Reuters via Kitco).R trifecta (OBBBA: Senate 50–50 with the Vice President's tie-break, House 218–214). The 2025 record reflects prior investment; the March 2026 spike to roughly $95 WTI followed strikes on Iran and a Hormuz shutdown (Reuters via Kitco); lease-sale effects lag years.

7.2

Electricity grid reliability and capacity

Measures whether generation and transmission keep pace with demand without blackouts; a failing grid is a national-security and economic-competitiveness liability.

AdministrationFactsDefenders sayCritics sayAttribution
DClinton (1993–2001)FERC Order 888 (Apr 1996) imposed open-access transmission tariffs and functional unbundling, launching wholesale competition (EIA archive); California's state-designed market saw summer-2000 price spikes that the CAISO market monitor attributed partly to suppliers exercising market power, prompting a FERC investigation (EIA archive); rolling blackouts hit California Jan 17–18, 2001 (PBS Frontline); reliability standards remained voluntary.Order 888 broke utility monopolies over transmission and created the RTO markets that later cut wholesale costs and integrated new resources.Deregulation without mandatory reliability authority or price safeguards set up the California crisis; FERC was slow to cap prices; no enforceable standards existed until 2007.R Congress 1995–2001. California's design was state law; a Western drought and gas-price spike drove the crisis, which straddled the Bush transition.
RBush 43 (2001–2009)Aug 14, 2003 blackout cascaded from Ohio line outages to more than 50 million people in the U.S. and Canada (ISO-NE); EPAct 2005 authorized FERC to certify an Electric Reliability Organization; NERC became the ERO and now enforces 100+ mandatory standards (ISO-NE, Wikipedia); FERC ordered generator refunds in Mar 2001 and limited price caps on Apr 25, 2001 (PBS Frontline).EPAct ended the voluntary-standards era with enforceable federal reliability rules and gave FERC backstop transmission siting.Standards took until 2007 to bite (approx.); backstop siting was never effectively used; the 2001 California intervention came late.R Congress through 2006 (EPAct 74–26, 275–156). The 2003 blackout stemmed from utility operations and tree contact (task force), not federal policy.
DObama (2009–2017)ARRA put $4.5B into grid modernization: 99 Smart Grid Investment Grant projects and 32 demonstrations (DOE); coal-related CO2 fell from 1,875 MMT (2009) to 1,355 MMT (2016) as gas displaced coal (EIA MER); Clean Power Plan (Aug 2015) was stayed by the Supreme Court Feb 9, 2016 and never took effect (Wikipedia); 2014 polar vortex strained eastern grids (approx.).Smart meters and synchrophasors modernized the grid; reliability held while nearly 20 GW of coal retired in Jan 2015–Apr 2016 alone (EIA) because cheap gas and wind filled in.EPA rules (mercury, Clean Power Plan) accelerated coal retirements that NERC later flagged as reliability risks; NERC warned of "overly rigid" policies (Utility Dive).D Congress passed ARRA. Retirements were driven chiefly by shale gas prices and plant age; the Clean Power Plan was stayed by the Court before it applied.
RTrump 45 (2017–2021)DOE's Sept 29, 2017 proposal sought cost-of-service payments for plants holding 90 days of on-site fuel (coal and nuclear); FERC rejected it Jan 8, 2018, finding no evidence existing tariffs were unjust, and opened a resilience docket (National Law Review); coal-related CO2 fell to 876 MMT in 2020 (EIA MER); California utilities began wildfire power shutoffs in 2019 (state).DOE flagged fuel-security risk years before NERC's warnings and forced FERC to open a resilience proceeding.The proposal was a bailout for uneconomic coal and nuclear plants; a Republican-majority FERC rejected it unanimously (National Law Review); retirements continued regardless.R Congress 2017–19. FERC, an independent agency, decided the outcome; markets drove retirements.
DBiden (2021–2025)Winter Storm Uri (Feb 2021) left 4.5 million Texas customers dark and killed 246; ERCOT sits outside FERC jurisdiction and had ignored 2011 winterization advice (Wikipedia); IIJA (Nov 2021) provided $73B for power infrastructure (Wikipedia); FERC Order 1920 (May 13, 2024, 2–1) required 20-year transmission planning (E&E News); NERC's 2023 assessment projected 83 GW of retirements in 10 years and a 4.7 GW MISO shortfall from 2028 (Utility Dive).Largest grid investment in history; Order 1920 finally broke the transmission planning logjam (E&E News).NERC warned that rigid environmental rules push generators to retire despite shortfall risk (Utility Dive); Commissioner Christie said Order 1920 exceeded FERC's authority and sidelined states (E&E News).D Congress 2021–23 (IIJA 69–30). Uri was a state-grid failure; FERC, not the White House, issued Order 1920; data-center load growth emerged in 2023–24.
RTrump 47 (2025– ) IN PROGRESSDOE Section 202(c) orders kept five coal plants (3.2 GW) running in 2025; coal retirements fell to 2.6 GW, the least since 2010 (EIA); DOE issued more than two dozen such orders in 15 months (S&P Global); DOE's July 7, 2025 report said blackout hours could rise 100-fold by 2030 if 104 GW retires (Utility Dive); NERC's 2025 assessment (Jan 29, 2026) projects summer peak demand up 224 GW in 10 years with 13 of 23 areas facing shortfalls (POWER); PJM (162.6 GW) and ERCOT (91.1 GW) set peak records in summer 2026 (EIA); in Sept 2026 the D.C. Circuit held DOE exceeded its 202(c) authority in the J.H. Campbell coal-plant order, and DOE sought rehearing Oct 26, 2026 (Sabin Center).Secretary Wright and America's Power: firm dispatchable capacity must stay online for AI-driven load; delaying retirements averts shortfalls (Utility Dive).Advanced Energy United, Sierra Club and Policy Integrity say the DOE report exaggerates risk and DOE's own study found no present emergency where orders were issued (Utility Dive); each 90-day order costs utilities millions (S&P Global).R trifecta. Demand growth (1.7%/yr 2020–25 vs 0.1% 2005–19) is data-center driven (EIA); 2025's 53 GW of additions were planned under prior law; reliability outcome data not yet available.

7.3

Nuclear energy

Measures reactor fleet size, new builds and regulatory capacity; nuclear supplies about a fifth of U.S. power and underpins fuel-cycle and naval security.

AdministrationFactsDefenders sayCritics sayAttribution
DClinton (1993–2001)No reactor has been ordered since 1978; Watts Bar 1 (licensed 1996) was the last completion for two decades (CRS); the fleet peaked at 112 in 1991 and fell to 104 by 1998 after closures such as Trojan (1992) (Wikipedia); the Integral Fast Reactor program was cancelled in 1994 (approx.); DOE missed its Jan 31, 1998 statutory date to begin taking spent fuel (CRS).Existing plants ran far better: capacity factors climbed, reaching 89% by 2009 (CRS), under license-renewal and performance oversight, and the 1992 licensing reforms were preserved.Cancelling the IFR and other advanced programs ceded technology leadership, the waste deadlock deepened, and no new build was attempted.R Congress 1995–2001. Cheap gas and deregulation made new nuclear uneconomic; the 1998 waste default stemmed from the 1987 Yucca law.
RBush 43 (2001–2009)EPAct 2005 created a 1.8-cent/kWh production credit for 6,000 MW, Title XVII loan guarantees (nuclear ceiling $18.5B) and standby support for six reactors; Nuclear Power 2010 cost-shared licensing; DOE filed the Yucca Mountain license application June 3, 2008; 104 reactors supplied about 20% of power (CRS).A "nuclear renaissance" package drew 17 combined-license applications for 26 units (CRS) and finally put Yucca before the NRC.EPAct's $4.3B nuclear incentives were criticized as subsidies to a mature industry (Wikipedia); no reactor broke ground; Yucca's cost and Nevada's opposition were unresolved.R Congress 2001–07. The 2008 crisis and the shale-gas price collapse undercut the economics; the applications it spurred were licensed in 2012.
DObama (2009–2017)Feb 16, 2010 conditional $8.33B loan guarantee for Vogtle 3–4; moved to withdraw the Yucca application Mar 3, 2010 and created the Blue Ribbon Commission (CRS); Vogtle and Summer construction began Mar 2013; five reactors (San Onofre 2–3, Crystal River, Kewaunee, Vermont Yankee) closed 2013–14; Watts Bar 2 entered service Oct 19, 2016 (Wikipedia).First new reactors licensed in 30+ years, backed by loan guarantees; nuclear counted as clean energy; consent-based siting replaced a dead-end repository fight.Ending Yucca without legal authority (an NRC panel rejected the withdrawal, CRS); no federal support as cheap gas closed plants; Vogtle overruns began on his watch.D Congress 2009–11, then divided. Yucca's termination tracked Sen. Reid's priorities; closures reflected gas prices and state market design.
RTrump 45 (2017–2021)Westinghouse's Mar 2017 bankruptcy preceded V.C. Summer's abandonment on July 31, 2017; Pilgrim closed May 2019 (Wikipedia); NEIMA (P.L. 115-439) enacted in 2019 (CRS); Oct 13, 2020 DOE awarded $160M to TerraPower and X-energy under a planned $3.2B seven-year Advanced Reactor Demonstration Program (DOE); FERC rejected DOE's coal-and-nuclear support proposal (National Law Review).Launched the advanced-reactor demonstrations, signed bipartisan licensing modernization and pressed fuel security.The fuel-security proposal was rejected as an unjustified bailout; five reactors closed between Sept 2018 and Aug 2020 (CRS) with no federal rescue; Yucca restart funding failed.R Congress 2017–19 passed NEIMA with bipartisan votes; ARDP was first funded ($230M) in the Dec 2019 appropriations law, P.L. 116-94 (CRS); closures were market-driven.
DBiden (2021–2025)Vogtle 3 (July 2023) and 4 (Apr 29, 2024) entered service about seven years late at roughly $35B vs a $14B estimate; regulators passed $7.56B to Georgia ratepayers (AJC); ADVANCE Act signed July 9, 2024 (E&E News); DOE offered up to $1.52B to restart Palisades (Michigan Public); IRA production credit for existing plants; a Civil Nuclear Credit award of up to $1.1B for Diablo Canyon (Jan 2024, CRS).Finished Vogtle, financed the first U.S. restart, signed bipartisan NRC reform and kept the fleet open with IRA credits.Vogtle's cost is a cautionary tale; critics note IRA loan funds and credit transferability were later targeted (CSIS); restarts slipped.Vogtle was licensed in 2012 and inherited; ADVANCE passed a divided Congress overwhelmingly; restart results lag into 2026.
RTrump 47 (2025– ) IN PROGRESSFour May 23, 2025 orders set 400 GW by 2050, 10 large reactors under construction by 2030 and 18-month NRC licensing caps (CSIS); fired NRC commissioner Hanson June 13, 2025 (AP via Click2Houston); Oct 28, 2025 $80B Westinghouse–Cameco–Brookfield AP1000 partnership with profit-sharing (Utility Dive); OBBBA kept nuclear credits plus a 10% bonus (Wikipedia); Palisades restart slipped past early 2026 (Michigan Public); fuel was reloaded in Oct 2026 and Secretary Wright said restart could come by end-Nov 2026 pending NRC clearance (Michigan Public); NRC regulates 94 reactors (NRC).The most ambitious nuclear agenda in decades: NRC culture reform, a fleet-scale AP1000 order and data-center demand finally align.CSIS: financing is the biggest hurdle, the FY26 budget cuts the nuclear office 25%, only two AP1000s are licensed (CSIS); UCS and Democrats call the Hanson firing an attack on independence (AP).R trifecta; builds on the 2024 ADVANCE Act. No new large reactor has started construction; outcome data not yet available.

7.4

Renewables and clean-energy deployment

Measures wind, solar and storage capacity added and their generation share; shapes the resource mix, supply chains and ratepayer costs.

AdministrationFactsDefenders sayCritics sayAttribution
DClinton (1993–2001)Inherited the wind production tax credit created in 1992 (Wikipedia); wind and solar supplied under 1% of U.S. electricity through 2005 (EIA); the PTC lapsed July 1–Dec 17, 1999 before a retroactive extension (CRS); Million Solar Roofs initiative (1997, approx.); installed wind was roughly 2.5 GW by 2000 (approx.). Domain barely applied: deployment was negligible.Seeded research (PV, wind, advanced vehicles) and kept the PTC alive; Kyoto signaled direction.Little was built; subsidies went to a politically favored, immature technology; Kyoto was unratifiable.R Congress 1995–2001 let the PTC lapse; cheap gas and immature technology, not policy, limited deployment.
RBush 43 (2001–2009)EPAct 2005 extended the PTC ($2.7B) and created Clean Renewable Energy Bonds (Wikipedia); the Oct 2008 bailout bill extended the solar credit eight years to 2016 (Wikipedia); EISA raised the biofuel mandate to 36B gallons by 2022 (CRS); wind reached 24.65 GW and grid solar 792 MW by 2008 (Wikipedia wind, Wikipedia solar).Created the modern solar credit and biofuel standard and extended the PTC; wind capacity grew several-fold.The corn-ethanol mandate raised food prices while cellulosic volumes never materialized; stop-start PTC cycles whipsawed investment.R Congress 2001–07; EISA and the 2008 extension passed a D Congress. Deployment tracked state renewable mandates and falling costs.
DObama (2009–2017)ARRA funded $6B in loan guarantees, $5B in weatherization and 30% cash grants (Section 1603, 2009–11) (Wikipedia ARRA, Wikipedia solar); Solyndra's $535M guarantee ended in a Sept 1, 2011 bankruptcy and $528M loss (Wikipedia); Dec 2015 omnibus extended the ITC/PTC with phase-downs; solar grew from 2.1 GW (2010) to 41.2 GW (2016) and wind from 24.65 GW (2008) to 81.3 GW (2016) (Wikipedia solar, Wikipedia wind).ARRA cut solar costs and the loan portfolio turned a profit overall despite Solyndra (Wikipedia); renewable generation roughly doubled.Solyndra typified picking winners with taxpayer money; 1603 grants and mandates distorted markets and raised rates.D Congress passed ARRA 2009; the 2015 extension was an R-Congress swap for the oil-export repeal. Chinese manufacturing drove the cost collapse.
RTrump 45 (2017–2021)Imposed 30% Section 201 solar tariffs in Jan 2018 (Wikipedia); PTC phase-down continued under 2015 law; solar reached 96.5 GW by 2020 (Wikipedia); wind added more than 14 GW in 2020 (EIA); renewables hit record output in 2019 (Trump White House archive); Vineyard Wind's federal review was extended (approx.).Renewable records were set without new mandates; tariffs defended domestic manufacturing (Trump White House archive).Tariffs raised project costs; offshore wind permitting stalled; Paris withdrawal isolated U.S. industry.Credits were fixed by 2015 law; state mandates and cost declines drove installs; a D House and R Senate extended the wind credit in the Dec 20, 2019 spending law (CRS).
DBiden (2021–2025)IRA (Aug 16, 2022) scored at $391B for energy and climate, later estimates $800B–$1.2T (Wikipedia); utility-scale solar additions hit a record 30.8 GW in 2024 (EIA); set a 30 GW offshore goal, yet only 174 MW was installed by Mar 2025 after Ørsted cancelled Ocean Wind in 2023 (Wikipedia); Vineyard Wind delivered first power Jan 2, 2024 (Wikipedia).$493B of clean investment in two years and 400,000 announced jobs (Wikipedia).Credits cost far more than scored; households over $100K took about 66% of 2023 residential credits (Wikipedia); offshore wind missed targets badly.50–50 Senate, party-line IRA. Deployment lags credits two to four years; offshore delays were inflation and interest-rate driven.
RTrump 47 (2025– ) IN PROGRESSJan 20, 2025 memo paused wind permitting (Wikipedia); Revolution Wind stop-work order (Aug 2025) lifted by injunction in Sept 2025 (Wikipedia); Dec 2025 pause on five offshore projects, enjoined Jan 2026 (Wikipedia); OBBBA ends wind/solar credits unless construction starts by mid-2026 or service by end-2027, and ends residential and EV credits in 2025 (Wikipedia); 2025 additions were 53 GW (27.2 solar, 15 battery), most since 2002, with a record 86 GW planned for 2026 (EIA); wind and solar reached 17% of generation in 2025 (EIA).Ending subsidies for "unreliable" wind and solar saves hundreds of billions and restores a level market; Interior's July 2025 review ends preferential treatment (AEA).Courts twice overrode stop-work orders on fully permitted projects; a post-2027 credit cliff threatens the pipeline just as demand surges.OBBBA was party-line. Record 2025–26 installs reflect pre-OBBBA safe-harbored projects; post-2027 outcome data not yet available.

7.5

Climate policy and greenhouse-gas emissions

Measures federal climate commitments and measured emissions; tests whether a state can honor treaties and manage long-run physical and trade risk.

AdministrationFactsDefenders sayCritics sayAttribution
DClinton (1993–2001)Senate passed Byrd-Hagel 95–0 on July 25, 1997; the administration signed Kyoto Nov 12, 1998 but never submitted it (Wikipedia); the 1993 BTU tax died in the Senate (approx.); coal-related CO2 rose from 1,882 MMT (1993) to 2,155 MMT (2000) (EIA MER).Put climate on the agenda, negotiated Kyoto and launched voluntary action plans while growing the economy.Signed a treaty the Senate had pre-rejected unanimously; emissions rose every year; policy was symbolic.Unanimous Senate opposition; 1990s growth drove emissions regardless of the White House.
RBush 43 (2001–2009)Rejected Kyoto on Mar 13, 2001 (Wikipedia); Massachusetts v. EPA (2007, 5–4) held greenhouse gases are Clean Air Act pollutants (Wikipedia); EISA set 35 mpg by 2020 (CRS); coal CO2 peaked at 2,180 MMT in 2005 (EIA MER); total U.S. emissions peaked in 2007 (approx.).Intensity targets, technology programs and EISA efficiency cut emissions growth without the economic cost of Kyoto.Eight years of delay; EPA sat on the endangerment question after the Court ruled; emissions peaked on his watch.R Congress 2001–07; EISA came from a D Congress. DObama (2009–2017)Endangerment finding Dec 2009 (Wikipedia); cap-and-trade (H.R. 2454) passed the House 219–212 on June 26, 2009 (House Clerk) but was never enacted; Clean Power Plan (Aug 3, 2015) targeted 32% below 2005 by 2030 and was stayed Feb 9, 2016 (Wikipedia); joined Paris Sept 2016 (Wikipedia); coal CO2 fell from 1,875 MMT (2009) to 1,355 MMT (2016) (EIA MER).Paris, the Clean Power Plan and doubled vehicle standards put the U.S. on a declining path.Regulatory overreach: the Court stayed the CPP and later rejected its theory; a "war on coal"; Paris bypassed the Senate.Cap-and-trade failed in a 60-seat D Senate; cuts came mostly from gas displacing coal; the CPP never applied.
RTrump 45 (2017–2021)Announced Paris withdrawal June 1, 2017, effective Nov 4, 2020 (Wikipedia); replaced the CPP with ACE on June 19, 2019, vacated by the D.C. Circuit Jan 19, 2021 (Wikipedia); the SAFE rule (Mar 31, 2020) set fuel-economy increases at 1.5% a year for model years 2021–26 (NHTSA); coal CO2 fell from 1,318 MMT (2017) to 876 MMT (2020) (EIA MER).Emissions fell anyway through markets and gas; Paris was unfair to U.S. workers.Rollbacks were vacated or reversed; the 2020 drop was COVID; the U.S. isolated itself diplomatically.Courts, not Congress, settled ACE; declines tracked gas prices and the pandemic.
DBiden (2021–2025)Rejoined Paris Feb 19, 2021 (Wikipedia); IRA modeled at 32–42% below 2005 by 2030 (Wikipedia); West Virginia v. EPA (June 30, 2022, 6–3) limited EPA (Wikipedia); net emissions in 2022 were 17% below 2005 (EPA); energy CO2 slipped from 4,906 MMT (2021) to 4,789 MMT (2024) (EIA).The largest climate law in history plus power-plant, methane and vehicle rules put a 40% cut within reach.Costs ballooned past estimates; rules amounted to an EV mandate; the Court signaled overreach; emissions fell only about 2% in three years.Party-line IRA; 2022 energy shock; courts constrained EPA; emissions track gas, coal and weather more than rules.
RTrump 47 (2025– ) IN PROGRESSParis withdrawal ordered Jan 20, 2025, effective Jan 27, 2026, with UNFCCC exit notified Feb 2026 (Wikipedia); EPA proposed repealing the endangerment finding July 29, 2025 and finalized it Feb 12, 2026, ending all vehicle GHG standards (Wikipedia); OBBBA ended EV credits and zeroed CAFE penalties (Wikipedia); energy CO2 rose 2% to 4,904 MMT in 2025 (EIA).Removes the legal basis for a costly regulatory agenda; the GHG reporting repeal alone saves up to $2.4B (AEA).The National Academies called the harm evidence "beyond scientific dispute"; a judge found the DOE climate panel violated FACA on Jan 30, 2026 (Wikipedia).Repeal is executive action facing litigation; the 2025 emissions rise reflects weather and data-center load (EIA); the D.C. Circuit denied a stay of the repeal Oct 5, 2026 and merits briefing is pending (Sabin Center).

Surface infrastructure (highways, bridges, transit, rail)

Measures the condition and funding of roads, bridges, transit and rail; logistics capacity and mobility underpin commerce and defense.

Bucket 1 of 10

Fiscal & Budget

  1. 1.1Annual deficits and surplusesUnified budget balance as a share of GDP — whether the state pays for what it spends or borrows the difference each year.
  2. 1.2National debt trajectory (debt held by public, % of GDP)Stock of federal debt owed to investors relative to the economy — the state's accumulated borrowing and its exposure to interest rates.
  3. 1.3Tax policy (rates, code structure, major tax laws)Statutory rates, the tax base and the major tax laws — how the state raises revenue and on whom the burden falls.
  4. 1.4Budget process discipline (regular order, appropriations on time, CRs/omnibuses)Whether the 12 appropriations bills are enacted by Oct 1 under binding budget rules, or by continuing resolutions, omnibuses and lapses.
  5. 1.5Entitlement solvency (Social Security & Medicare trust-fund dates and reforms)Projected depletion dates of the Social Security (OASI/OASDI) and Medicare HI trust funds and the laws that moved them — the state's ability to honor promised benefits.
  6. 1.6Debt-ceiling managementHow the statutory borrowing limit was raised or suspended and whether brinkmanship imposed costs — a sovereign's reliability as a borrower.
  7. 1.7Government shutdowns and continuity of operationsFunding lapses that halted operations, their length and measured cost — whether the state can keep itself running through political disputes.
  8. 1.8Federal workforce size and managementHeadcount of the executive-branch civilian workforce (excluding the Postal Service) and the management regime governing it — the state's administrative capacity.
  9. 1.9Net interest cost on the debtFederal net interest outlays in dollars and as a share of GDP — the slice of revenue consumed by past borrowing before any current need is met.
  10. 1.10Emergency and stimulus spending and its accountability (TARP, ARRA, CARES, ARP, etc.)Size, design and oversight of emergency fiscal interventions — whether the state can act fast in a crisis and account for the money afterward.
Bucket 2 of 10

Economy, Jobs & Monetary

  1. 2.1Real GDP growthInflation-adjusted output growth; the base for jobs, tax revenue and strategic capacity.
  2. 2.2Unemployment and labor-force participationShare of the labor force without work and share of adults in it; the clearest test of whether growth reaches people.
  3. 2.3Inflation and price stabilityChange in the consumer price level; it decides whether nominal gains are real and whether the dollar keeps trust.
  4. 2.4Real wages and real median household incomeInflation-adjusted pay and household income at the median; whether the typical family is getting ahead.
  5. 2.5Poverty rate (official and supplemental)Share below the official threshold and under the SPM, which counts taxes and benefits; the floor a state keeps under its citizens.
  6. 2.6Income and wealth inequalityDispersion of income (Gini) and concentration of wealth; it shapes social cohesion and who bears the cost of shocks.
  7. 2.7Federal Reserve independence and appointmentsWho runs the central bank and whether the White House lets it set rates; the anchor of dollar credibility.
  8. 2.8Financial regulation and banking stabilityRules on banks and markets and whether they prevent failures; the difference between a recession and a systemic crisis.
  9. 2.9Housing affordability and homeownershipHome prices and mortgage rates relative to incomes, and the share of households that own; the main store of middle-class wealth.
  10. 2.10Small-business climate and new-business formationConditions for starting and running small firms and the rate of new-business applications; the economy's renewal mechanism.
Bucket 3 of 10

National Security & Defense

  1. 3.1Defense budget and force readinessMeasures how much the state spends on its military and whether that force is trained, equipped and ready to fight when called.
  2. 3.2Major wars and interventionsMeasures when, how and with what authority the state commits force abroad, and what those wars cost in lives, money and outcomes.
  3. 3.3CounterterrorismMeasures the state's ability to prevent attacks on its people and dismantle terrorist networks while staying within law it can defend.
  4. 3.4Nuclear posture and arms controlMeasures how the state limits, modernizes and deters with the weapons that can end it, and whether treaties constrain its rivals.
  5. 3.5Veterans' care and the VAMeasures whether the state delivers timely health care and earned benefits to those it sent to war, and how it handles the bill.
  6. 3.6Intelligence community oversight and reformMeasures whether the state's spies serve policy without politicization, and whether Congress and courts can check them.
  7. 3.7Cybersecurity and critical-infrastructure defenseMeasures whether the state can protect its networks, utilities and secrets from foreign intrusion and respond when they fail.
  8. 3.8Space policy (NASA, Space Force, commercial space)Measures whether the state keeps access to and advantage in orbit — civil exploration, military space, and a commercial launch base.
  9. 3.9Military recruitment, retention and personnel policyMeasures whether the state can fill and keep an all-volunteer force, and how personnel rules shape who serves and who leads.
  10. 3.10Defense industrial base and procurement reformMeasures whether the state can buy weapons on time and budget and surge production in war, and whether reform laws change outcomes.
Bucket 4 of 10

Foreign Policy & Alliances

  1. 4.1NATO and European alliances (incl. Ukraine)Measures alliance cohesion, burden-sharing, enlargement and support for Ukraine — the main instrument of U.S. power in Europe.
  2. 4.2China strategyMeasures how each administration balanced trade, deterrence and engagement with the principal peer competitor.
  3. 4.3Russia relationsMeasures management of the one adversary with a comparable nuclear arsenal — arms control, sanctions, deterrence and diplomacy.
  4. 4.4Middle East, Israel and the PalestiniansMeasures the U.S. role in Arab–Israeli peace and regional security — commitments, aid and conflict outcomes.
  5. 4.5IranMeasures efforts to prevent an Iranian bomb and counter its proxies — sanctions, diplomacy and force.
  6. 4.6North KoreaMeasures efforts to halt a hostile nuclear program — the one case where every approach has been tried.
  7. 4.7Western Hemisphere (Mexico, Cuba, Venezuela, Central America)Measures management of the near abroad — trade, drugs, migration and the Cuban and Venezuelan regimes.
  8. 4.8Foreign aid and soft power (USAID, PEPFAR, Millennium Challenge)Measures aid volume, effectiveness and the institutions that carry U.S. influence abroad.
  9. 4.9International institutions and treaties (UN, WTO, Paris, WHO, ICC)Measures whether the U.S. binds itself to the rules it helped write — treaties ratified, bodies joined or left.
  10. 4.10Indo-Pacific allies and partners (Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Australia, India, Philippines)Measures alliance strength with the treaty allies and partners that anchor U.S. position against China.
Bucket 5 of 10

Borders, Immigration & Citizenship

  1. 5.1Border security and enforcement (apprehensions, barriers, agents)Measures whether the state controls who enters between ports of entry: Border Patrol apprehensions, barrier miles and agent strength — the most basic test of territorial sovereignty.
  2. 5.2Legal immigration levels and visa policyMeasures how many people the state admits permanently and on what terms — the sovereign choice of who joins the polity, set by the 1990 Act's ~675,000 flexible cap.
  3. 5.3Interior enforcement and removals/deportationsMeasures whether immigration law is enforced inside the country — formal removals, returns, detention — the follow-through that gives border rules meaning.
  4. 5.4Asylum and refugee policyMeasures how the state honors (or limits) its treaty and statutory duty to protect the persecuted without letting the asylum channel substitute for immigration control.
  5. 5.5Unauthorized population and legalization efforts (1996 law, 2006/2013 bills, DACA/DAPA)Measures the size of the population living outside the law and whether the political system resolved its status — the unfinished business of every presidency since 1986.
  6. 5.6Skilled and employment-based immigration (H-1B, green-card backlog)Measures whether the state's rules for high-skill workers serve its labor market and innovation base — a 65,000 H-1B cap and 140,000 employment green cards, both set in 1990.
  7. 5.7Immigration courts and case backlogMeasures whether the adjudication system can decide who stays within a useful time — a backlog converts every border policy into de facto admission or de facto denial.
  8. 5.8Naturalization, citizenship and birthright-citizenship policyMeasures how the state admits people to full membership — naturalization volume and integrity, and the constitutional rule for citizenship at birth.
  9. 5.9Cooperation with Mexico and Canada on migration and border tradeMeasures whether the state can enlist its two land neighbors — its largest trading partners — to manage migration and crime without wrecking the commerce that crosses the same line.
  10. 5.10Smuggling, trafficking and fentanyl interdiction at the borderMeasures whether the state can stop the criminal flows — people-smuggling, trafficking and synthetic drugs — that exploit the same border, the lethal end of immigration policy.
Bucket 6 of 10

Trade, Industry & Technology

  1. 6.1Trade agreements (NAFTA/USMCA, WTO accession, KORUS, TPP, Phase One)Measures the binding trade pacts each administration negotiated or ratified; they set the rules under which a state's exporters, importers and workers compete.
  2. 6.2Tariffs and trade enforcement (Section 232/301, IEEPA tariffs)Measures use of tariffs and trade-remedy law; tariffs are a state's most direct lever on imports, prices and foreign leverage.
  3. 6.3Goods and services trade deficitMeasures the gap between what the country sells and buys abroad (Census/BEA FT-900), a marker of competitiveness and dependence on foreign capital.
  4. 6.4Manufacturing employment and reshoringMeasures factory jobs (BLS CES) and new plant investment; manufacturing capacity underpins defense, supply-chain security and middle-income work.
  5. 6.5Industrial policy (CHIPS, IRA, Buy American, strategic sectors)Measures direct state support for chosen industries, which decides whether critical goods (chips, batteries, steel, vaccines) can be made at home.
  6. 6.6Federal R&D and technology leadershipMeasures federal science funding and its outcomes; sustained public research underwrites the technologies on which military and economic primacy rest.
  7. 6.7Antitrust and Big TechMeasures enforcement against monopoly and merger power, especially in technology; it determines whether markets, not incumbents, allocate the economy's gains.
  8. 6.8Artificial-intelligence policyMeasures how each administration governed and promoted AI; it is now the contested frontier of economic and military advantage.
  9. 6.9Export controls and intellectual-property protectionMeasures controls on sensitive technology exports and defense of patents and copyrights; both guard the technological edge a state depends on.
  10. 6.10Agriculture and farm policy (farm bills, subsidies, trade retaliation payments)Measures farm-income support and the fallout of trade fights on growers; food production and rural economies are a strategic base for any state.
Bucket 7 of 10

Energy, Infrastructure & Environment

  1. 7.1Domestic oil and gas production and energy independenceMeasures crude and gas output and the net energy trade position; determines price exposure, sanctions leverage and vulnerability to foreign supply shocks.
  2. 7.2Electricity grid reliability and capacityMeasures whether generation and transmission keep pace with demand without blackouts; a failing grid is a national-security and economic-competitiveness liability.
  3. 7.3Nuclear energyMeasures reactor fleet size, new builds and regulatory capacity; nuclear supplies about a fifth of U.S. power and underpins fuel-cycle and naval security.
  4. 7.4Renewables and clean-energy deploymentMeasures wind, solar and storage capacity added and their generation share; shapes the resource mix, supply chains and ratepayer costs.
  5. 7.5Climate policy and greenhouse-gas emissionsMeasures federal climate commitments and measured emissions; tests whether a state can honor treaties and manage long-run physical and trade risk.
  6. 7.6Surface infrastructure (highways, bridges, transit, rail)Measures the condition and funding of roads, bridges, transit and rail; logistics capacity and mobility underpin commerce and defense.
  7. 7.7Broadband and digital infrastructureMeasures home-broadband reach and federal deployment programs; connectivity is now prerequisite for commerce, schooling, telehealth and emergency communication.
  8. 7.8Water infrastructure and drinking-water safetyMeasures safe-drinking-water rules, lead and PFAS controls and pipe funding; water failures are immediate public-health and legitimacy crises.
  9. 7.9Public lands, conservation and permitting reform (NEPA)Measures protected acreage, monument decisions and the speed of federal environmental review; both resource access and conservation depend on it.
  10. 7.10Disaster response and resilience (FEMA, major hurricanes, wildfires)Measures federal readiness and response to catastrophes; it is the most visible test of state capacity citizens experience directly.
Bucket 8 of 10

Rule of Law, Institutions & Civil Liberties

  1. 8.1Judicial appointments and the federal courtsWho staffs the Article III bench and how the Senate confirms them; the courts decide the legality of everything else in this scorecard.
  2. 8.2Executive power and emergency authoritiesHow far presidents act without Congress through orders, emergencies, war powers and statutory stretch; the core question of who governs between elections.
  3. 8.3Separation of powers and congressional oversightWhether Congress can obtain information, control spending and remove or protect officers; the practical test of checks and balances.
  4. 8.4Surveillance and privacy (FISA, Patriot Act, Section 702)The legal limits on government collection of Americans' communications; the balance between counterterrorism and the Fourth Amendment.
  5. 8.5Free speech and press freedomGovernment pressure on speakers, publishers and dissenters, measured by prosecutions, access disputes and external indices; a free press is how citizens check the state.
  6. 8.6Second Amendment and firearms policyFederal gun laws, their enforcement and the constitutional boundary set by the courts; a core liberty for some citizens and a safety question for all.
  7. 8.7Criminal justice and incarcerationFederal sentencing, prison population, clemency and the death penalty; how a state uses its power to punish.
  8. 8.8Civil-rights enforcementHow DOJ and agencies enforce anti-discrimination law in voting, policing, employment and education; the state's promise of equal protection.
  9. 8.9Government ethics, transparency and corruptionConflicts of interest, self-dealing, pardons for allies, and the watchdogs that police them; whether office is used for public or private ends.
  10. 8.10Independence of DOJ/FBI and politicization of law enforcementWhether prosecutions and investigations follow evidence or presidential preference; the line between law enforcement and political power.
Bucket 9 of 10

Public Health, Safety & Social Insurance

  1. 9.1Health-insurance coverage and costs (HIPAA, Medicare Part D, ACA, repeal efforts)Share of residents without coverage and what coverage costs; a state's ability to keep its workforce healthy and solvent against medical shocks.
  2. 9.2Medicare and Medicaid policyDesign, financing and enrollment of the two programs that cover roughly 150 million people and a third of federal spending.
  3. 9.3Prescription-drug pricesWhat Americans and federal programs pay for medicines; a lever on household budgets, Medicare solvency and the pharmaceutical industrial base.
  4. 9.4Pandemic preparedness and response (H1N1, Ebola, COVID-19)Stockpiles, surveillance, vaccines and the speed and credibility of federal response when a pathogen arrives.
  5. 9.5Opioid and fentanyl overdose crisisAnnual overdose deaths and the federal response to prescription opioids, heroin and illicit fentanyl.
  6. 9.6Life expectancy and mortality trendsLife expectancy at birth and the age-adjusted death rate — the summary scorecard of a nation's health system, behaviors and shocks.
  7. 9.7Violent crime and federal policing policyThe rate of murder and violent crime and the federal role in funding, policing and public order.
  8. 9.8K-12 education policy (NCLB, Race to the Top, ESSA, Dept. of Education)Federal leverage over 50 million public-school students and what national assessments show they learned.
  9. 9.9Higher education and student debtFederal aid, loan programs and the $1.7 trillion student-debt portfolio the Treasury owns.
  10. 9.10Social Security and retirement securitySolvency of the retirement and disability trust funds and the adequacy and administration of benefits for 70 million people.
Bucket 10 of 10

Elections, Civic Trust & Federalism

  1. 10.1Election administration and election securityWhether the federal government funds, defends and stays neutral toward the state-run machinery that produces results citizens accept.
  2. 10.2Voting rights and ballot accessHow far federal law protects the franchise against state ballot rules, and how the courts have reset that balance since 1993.
  3. 10.3Campaign financeRules on money in federal elections, the FEC's capacity to enforce them, and total spending — proxies for whether officeholders answer to voters or donors.
  4. 10.4Peaceful transfer of power and acceptance of resultsWhether losers concede, winners are seated on schedule, and officeholders accept certified results — the minimum condition for a sovereign republic.
  5. 10.5Public trust in government and institutionsThe Pew/NES trust-in-government series and Gallup's confidence-in-institutions average — the legitimacy reserve a state draws on in a crisis.
  6. 10.6Bipartisanship record and polarizationWhether landmark laws passed with cross-party majorities, and how far party medians in Congress and the public have diverged.
  7. 10.7Federalism and state–federal relationsWhere power sits between Washington and the states — mandates, preemption, conditional funding, and the use of federal force inside states.
  8. 10.8Census and apportionmentWhether the decennial count is accurate, on time and insulated from politics — it allocates House seats, electoral votes and trillions in federal funds.
  9. 10.9Civil service and the merit systemWhether the roughly two-million-person career workforce is hired and fired on merit, and who controls it — Congress, the president or neither.
  10. 10.10Media environment, misinformation and government–platform relationsHow each administration treated the press, public broadcasting and platforms — pressure, subsidy, prosecution or "jawboning" — and what courts allowed.
